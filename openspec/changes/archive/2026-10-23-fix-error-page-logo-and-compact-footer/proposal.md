## Why

On the router's last-resort error page ("Something went wrong"), the logo
shows only "Gu" in the header and footer. The "estify" part of the
wordmark is missing. The cause is the theme. `RouteErrorPage` renders
outside `PublicLayout`, so it never forces the light theme that every
other public page uses. A user with Dark stored, or with System on a dark
device, gets dark surfaces. The light-surface logo's navy (`#12172A`)
wordmark then disappears into them. The staff phone navigation bar
(`AppShell`'s `MobileNavBar`) has the same problem: it shows the
light-surface logo on `bg-surface` while following the theme.

The shared portal footer is also too tall for what it holds. It is
logo + tagline + copyright stacked, with 32–40px padding, about 180px on
desktop. On a short page like the error page it takes about a fifth of
the screen.

## What Changes

- The route error page renders in the light theme, like the 404 page and
  every other public page, whatever the stored theme or device setting.
- A light-surface logo that sits on a themed surface switches to the
  white-wordmark lockup while the dark theme is active. This fixes the
  staff phone navigation bar, and it is a safety net for any other themed
  surface that uses the standard logo.
- The shared `PortalFooter` becomes compact on every public page (visitor
  portal, visitor request, tracking, 404, error page):
  - less vertical padding;
  - on desktop, the logo, tagline and copyright sit on one row, beside
    the links;
  - on phones the blocks still stack, with tighter spacing.

  The logo stays 28px tall. The tagline, copyright, Privacy/Terms links
  and support email are all kept.

### Modified Capabilities
- `theme-mode`: "Public pages always render in the light theme" now
  explicitly covers the not-found page and the route error page. The
  error page must hold that even though it renders outside the app's
  providers.
- `brand-identity`: adds a requirement that the logo stays fully legible
  in the dark theme. A light-surface logo on a themed surface swaps to the
  white wordmark.
- `responsive-layout`: adds a requirement that the public portal footer
  is compact. It has a height cap on desktop and stays a tight stack on
  phones.
